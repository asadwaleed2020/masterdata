Takes the lower of 20 × monthly income and the loan present value supported by remaining headroom under 50% DBR. Lender affordability, credit record, employer criteria and product rules can reduce it substantially.

Two limits decide how much you can borrow

  • The salary multiple: a personal loan cannot exceed 20 times your monthly salary or total income.
  • The payment limit: your new instalment plus existing debts must stay within 50% of gross income, over a maximum of 48 months.

The lower of the two is your regulatory ceiling. The bank's own credit policy can bring it lower still.

Here the DBR test is lower, so the ceiling is about AED 208,800. With no existing debt, the 20× rule (AED 240,000) would be the limit instead.

What else banks check

  • Your AECB credit report and score.
  • Length of service with your employer, and whether your employer is on the bank's approved list.
  • Whether you will transfer your salary to the bank.
  • Residence visa and Emirates ID validity.
  • Your age at the end of the loan.
Avoid "guaranteed approval" agents. No legitimate agent can guarantee a bank decision. Apply directly through the bank's official channels.

Frequently asked questions

How much loan can I get with AED 10,000 salary in the UAE?

The 20× salary rule gives a maximum of AED 200,000. The 50% DBR rule may lower it: with no other debts and a 7% reducing rate over 48 months, AED 5,000 a month supports about AED 208,800, so the 20× limit of AED 200,000 applies. Banks can offer less.

Does the bank decide my eligibility or the Central Bank?

The Central Bank sets the maximum limits. Each bank decides whether to lend and how much, based on its credit policy.

Can I borrow more with a longer term?

For personal loans, no more than 48 months is allowed. A longer car loan (up to 60 months) or mortgage (up to 25 years) has its own limits.

Does a co-applicant help?

Some products, such as mortgages and car loans, may allow joint applications. Ask the bank how it counts joint income and debt.
