What is the 50% DBR rule?

Your debt burden ratio is your total monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. Under Central Bank rules, repayments for all loans at all banks must not exceed 50% of your regular income.

DBR = (all monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income) × 100

What banks count

ItemCounted?Note

Worked example

Monthly salary AED 15,000. Existing car loan AED 1,800. Card payments assessed at AED 1,500.

  • Current DBR: (1,800 + 1,500) ÷ 15,000 = 22%.
  • 50% of salary: AED 7,500.
  • Room for a new payment: 7,500 − 3,300 = AED 4,200 a month at the very most.

A bank can approve less. Staying near 50% leaves little room for rent and family costs, so many people aim much lower.

Tip: Closing an unused credit card, or lowering its limit, can improve your DBR if your lender counts card limits.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum DBR in the UAE?

50%. Central Bank rules say total loan repayments at all banks must not exceed 50% of the borrower's regular income.

Do credit cards count in DBR?

Yes. Credit card repayment must stay within the 50% limit. Some banks assess a share of your full card limit even if you owe nothing.

Is rent included in DBR?

No. DBR covers debt repayments. Banks still review whether you can afford living costs.

Does DBR use gross or net salary?

Gross salary and any verifiable regular income, according to the Central Bank regulation.

How can I lower my DBR?

Repay or close a loan, reduce or cancel unused card limits, or choose a longer term within the legal maximum, which lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.
