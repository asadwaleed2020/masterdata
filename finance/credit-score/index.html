What is the AECB credit score?

The Al Etihad Credit Bureau (AECB) is the UAE federal credit bureau. It collects data from banks, finance companies and other organisations, such as telecoms and utility providers, and produces your credit report and score.

The score runs from 300 to 900. A higher score means lower risk to lenders. AECB itself confirms the range; the bands below are commonly used in the market, not an official AECB label.

ScoreCommonly described as
746–900Very high / excellent
711–745High
651–710Medium / good
541–650Low / fair
300–540Very low

How to check your score

  1. Open the AECB website or app and log in with UAE PASS.
  2. Choose credit score, credit report, or both.
  3. Pay the fee shown in the app. Prices are set by AECB and have changed over time, so check the current amount there.
  4. Download your report and check every loan, card and cheque record.
Protect your data: IonicRide never asks for your Emirates ID or UAE PASS details. Use only the official AECB channels.

What lowers your score, and what helps

Lowers your scoreHelps your score
Late or missed loan and card paymentsPaying every bill on time
Bounced chequesUsing a small share of your card limits
Many credit applications in a short timeKeeping older accounts in good standing
Unpaid telecom or utility billsClosing debts fully and checking they show as closed

If a record is wrong, raise a dispute with AECB through its official correction process and contact the bank or company that reported it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good credit score in the UAE?

AECB scores run from 300 to 900. Banks often treat scores above about 700 as good, but each lender sets its own criteria.

How do I check my credit score in the UAE?

Use the Al Etihad Credit Bureau website or app and log in with UAE PASS. A fee applies, shown in the app.

Do unpaid phone bills affect my credit score?

They can. AECB collects payment information from organisations such as telecoms and utilities as well as banks.

Can someone remove negative records from my report?

Accurate records cannot simply be erased. Avoid anyone offering this. If a record is wrong, use the AECB dispute process.

Does checking my own score lower it?

Checking your own report through AECB is not a loan application. Many loan applications in a short time can be a negative sign to lenders.
