Everyone fixates on Xiaomi in the UAE, but Segway quietly builds some of the smartest commuters going — and the F3 is the one that stands out on paper. The pitch is almost cheeky: a 70 km-class long-ranger that weighs just 18.6 kg. that’s lighter than scooters with half the battery.

So is it the perfect Dubai commuter? Nearly. It’s light, it’s loaded with premium tech — a TFT screen that does turn-by-turn navigation, Apple Find My, self-sealing tyres, and the best water resistance in its class. But it has one real weakness that testers keep hitting, and at around AED 2,999 it’s not cheap.

This review covers range expectations in UAE heat, the light frame, the TFT and smart features, the hill-climbing catch nobody advertises, how it compares to the Xiaomis, and a clear verdict on whether it earns its price.

Research-based verdict: The F3 combines a relatively light published weight with a navigation display, self-sealing tyres and strong water-resistance specifications. Its premium price and reported limitations on sustained steep climbs make it a better fit for flatter commutes than hilly routes.

Who the Segway F3 Is Actually For

The F3 is built for the rider who wants long range without the usual weight penalty. Most 70 km scooters — the Xiaomi 6 Max included — pay for that battery in kilos, tipping the scales near 25–30 kg. The F3 pulls off nearly the same range at 18.6 kg, which changes the daily experience completely: it’s the one you can actually carry up a stairwell, fold into a car boot, or lift onto the Metro without a groan. In practical terms, if your commute mixes riding with carrying, that’s a big deal.

It’s also for the tech-minded rider. The colour TFT display, turn-by-turn navigation, Apple Find My and app connectivity make it feel a class above the plain commuters. Where it’s not the right pick: steep, hilly routes, and tight budgets — more on both below.

Range Expectations Beyond the Lab Claim

Segway publishes a maximum range of 70.8 km under its stated test conditions. That is useful for comparing models, but it is not a promise for every rider or route.

Speed, rider weight, wind, hills, tyre pressure and high temperatures can reduce distance between charges. Plan with a substantial reserve until you have measured your own route. Segway also lists an optional external battery for riders who need more capacity.

Ride, Tech & Build Quality

The F3 feels like a premium bit of kit, because it is one. The bright colour TFT display is the standout — connect your phone and it shows turn-by-turn navigation right on the dash, which is a treat for exploring the city without stopping to check your phone. You also get Apple Find My (locate it like an AirTag), cruise control, three ride modes, and a dedicated lock loop.

Build quality is a step up too — a magnesium alloy frame, 10″ self-sealing tubeless tyres that reseal small punctures, and the best weatherproofing in the class: IPX6 on the body and an impressive IPX7 on the battery, so a sudden Dubai downpour doesn’t faze it. Front hydraulic suspension takes the edge off bumps, though the rear elastomer setup and 10″ tyres make the ride a touch firmer than the big-tyre Xiaomis — comfortable, but not plush.

Hill-Climbing Trade-Off

Segway publishes a maximum slope rating for the F3, but hill performance depends heavily on rider weight, battery charge, speed, gradient length and temperature. Some published reviews report weaker performance on sustained steep climbs than the headline rating may suggest.

⛰️ What this means

For a mostly flat commute, this may not matter. If your route includes long ramps or steep gradients, buy from a seller with a clear return policy and confirm performance for your use case rather than relying on the maximum slope figure alone.

🔧 Other trade-offs

The brakes are mechanical rather than hydraulic, and the premium price reflects the light frame and connected features. Compare service access, parts and warranty as carefully as the specification sheet.

Before Riding in the UAE

A scooter’s advertised top speed is a product specification, not permission to ride at that speed. Rules vary by emirate, route and designated zone. In Dubai, the official RTA e-scooter page says riders need a permit in specified areas and must follow the published safety and track rules.

📋

Check the responsible local authority immediately before riding, use the permitted mode and speed, wear a helmet, obey signs and keep away from routes where e-scooters are not authorised.

So… Should You Buy the Segway F3?

✅ Buy it if…
  • You want long range in a light scooter you can actually carry — nothing else here is 18.6 kg with 70 km.
  • You value tech — TFT navigation, Apple Find My and top weatherproofing.
  • Your route is mostly flat (i.e. most of the UAE), so the hill weakness never bites.
  • You mix riding with stairs, Metro or car-boot carrying daily.
⛔ Skip it if…
  • Your route has steep, sustained climbs — get the Xiaomi 6 Max instead.
  • You want the most comfort — big-tyre Xiaomis ride softer.
  • You’re on a budget — the 4 Ultra gets close for ~AED 1,000 less.
📋 Bottom Line — Segway Ninebot F3 Review 2026

The Segway F3 pulls off a clever trick: 70 km-class range in an 18.6 kg scooter, wrapped in the best tech and weatherproofing in its bracket. For a flat-city commuter who carries their scooter, it’s close to ideal.

Research-based verdict: the main trade-offs are the premium price and reports of weaker performance on sustained steep climbs. Confirm both against your route and retailer return policy.

Our take: if your roads are flat and you value light weight and smart features, buy it and enjoy — it’s superb. If your route is steep or your budget is tight, the Xiaomi 6 Max or 4 Ultra is the wiser money. whichever you pick, check the current rules for the exact route and use appropriate protective equipment.

Segway Ninebot F3 — Full FAQ

Is the Segway Ninebot F3 worth considering in the UAE?

It is worth comparing if low weight, a navigation display, self-sealing tyres and published water-resistance ratings matter to you. Consider the premium price, route gradients, warranty and return terms before buying.

What range should F3 riders plan for?

Segway publishes up to 70.8 km under stated test conditions. Actual distance varies with speed, rider weight, wind, hills, tyre pressure and temperature, so leave a substantial reserve until you have measured your own route.

How much does the Segway F3 cost in the UAE?

Around AED 2,999 on Amazon.ae at the time of writing — one of the pricier 25 km/h commuters. Prices move, so confirm at checkout.

Can the Segway F3 be ridden in Dubai?

The scooter may be sold with a regional top-speed setting, but riding permission depends on the place and current rules. In specified Dubai areas, the RTA requires an e-scooter permit and publishes rider obligations. Check the official RTA guidance before every route.

Is the Segway F3 good at climbing hills?

It’s rated for around a 20% slope, but published testing reports indicate it can struggle and cut out on steep, sustained climbs. On the UAE’s mostly flat roads that’s rarely an issue, but for long steep gradients a more powerful scooter is safer.

Does the Segway F3 have self-sealing tyres?

Yes — 10-inch self-sealing tubeless tyres that reseal small punctures, reducing the most common roadside breakdown. It also has IPX6 body and an impressive IPX7 battery water rating.
