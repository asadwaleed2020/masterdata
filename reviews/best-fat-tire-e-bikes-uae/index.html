Best Fat-Tire E-Bikes in the UAE: 5 Models Compared on Speed, Weight and Dubai Rules

Quick answer

Most fat-tire e-bikes sold in the UAE can go much faster than Dubai’s cycle lanes allow. If you want to stay close to Dubai lane speeds without changing settings, look first at models listed with a 25 km/h limit, such as the ENGWE M20 or the folding ENGWE Engine Pro 2.0, which you can buy on Noon and Amazon.ae. If you are a heavier rider and need a big battery, the Kugoo V6 lists the largest battery and a 150 kg load, but it is also the heaviest bike here.

Fat tyres look great for the UAE. Sand on the edge of paths, rough service roads, broken tarmac. A 4-inch tyre handles all of that better than a thin commuter tyre.

The problem is what comes with the fat tyres. Many fat-tire e-bikes in UAE shops are sold with 750W to 1000W motors and top speeds of 50 to 60 km/h. That is closer to a light motorbike than a bicycle, and it does not match how Dubai regulates e-bikes.

This guide compares five fat-tire e-bikes that are listed by UAE retailers, explains the Dubai rules that matter for this type of bike, and gives you a checklist to use before you pay.

How this comparison was made: IonicRide has not ridden or tested these bikes. Specifications come from UAE retailer listings and manufacturer pages, checked in September and October 2026. Where sources disagree, we say so. Prices change often, so check the retailer before buying.

Before you buy: what Dubai’s rules mean for fat-tire e-bikes

In Dubai, e-bikes are covered by Executive Council Resolution No. 13 of 2022. It defines an electric bike as a cycle with an electric motor that can be powered by the motor or by pedalling. The resolution itself does not set a maximum motor wattage. What it does control matters more for fat-tire bikes.

Rules for the bike

Any cycle used on cycle lanes or RTA-designated areas must have:

  • a white headlight, a red tail light and a red rear reflector
  • a bell on the handlebar
  • front and rear brakes
  • tyres suitable for the road or lane you ride on

The resolution also says riders must not add major parts or make substantial changes to their cycle. Keep that in mind before any “speed unlock”.

Rules for the rider

  • The resolution sets a minimum age of 16 for e-bikes.
  • Ride only on cycle lanes designated by the RTA.
  • Do not ride an e-bike on any road with a speed limit above 60 km/h.
  • Follow the speed limits the RTA sets for each lane. These are shown on signs.
  • Wear a helmet and a reflective vest.

For repeat violations that threaten safety, the RTA can also impound the cycle for 30 days. The newer federal traffic law (Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2024) also covers light mobility devices, so penalties may change. Our Dubai fines guide tracks updates.

The speed problem

A bike that can reach 50 or 60 km/h is not illegal to own, but you can only legally use a small part of that speed in Dubai’s cycle lanes. Riding fast on shared paths is a fine risk and a real danger to pedestrians and other riders. IonicRide’s view: in Dubai, a 25 km/h bike with good tyres and brakes is more useful than a 60 km/h bike you cannot legally ride at speed.

Rules outside Dubai are different. See our guides for Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, and our guide to riding e-bikes on Dubai’s cycle paths. If a model looks more like a moped than a bicycle, read whether electric mopeds are legal in Dubai before you buy.

ENGWE M20: best for staying close to legal speeds out of the box

The M20 is a moped-style fat-tire e-bike with a long seat and 20 × 4.0-inch tyres. According to a UAE retailer listing (E-Scooter UAE Hub, checked September 2026), the version sold here has a 750W motor, 55 Nm of torque and a 48V 13Ah battery, with a 26Ah dual-battery option. It ships limited to 25 km/h, and the listing says the speed can be unlocked.

Listed range: 55 km on throttle only (mode 1) and 75 km with pedal assist (mode 1). Real range in UAE heat, with a heavier rider or more throttle, will usually be lower. That is an IonicRide estimate, not a figure from the retailer.

What stands out

  • 25 km/h default is the closest match to Dubai lane speeds in this list
  • Shimano 7-speed gears and 160 mm disc brakes front and rear
  • Dual-battery version for longer rides
  • 1-year manufacturer warranty stated by the retailer

Watch out for

  • 34.8 kg is heavy to carry up stairs
  • 120 kg maximum load is the lowest here
  • Listed maximum climb of 10°, fine for bridges, not steep hills
  • Most expensive of the bikes with a UAE price seen (AED 4,890)

Good fit for: riders under 120 kg who want a comfortable, stable bike for lanes and light gravel, and who do not plan to unlock the speed.

ENGWE Engine Pro 2.0: best folding fat-tire pick you can order easily

The Engine Pro 2.0 is ENGWE’s current folding fat-tyre e-bike, and it is listed on both Noon and Amazon.ae. According to ENGWE’s published European specifications, it has a 750W motor (900W peak) with 75 Nm of torque, a 52V 16Ah battery (832Wh), 20 × 4.0-inch tyres, hydraulic disc brakes front and rear, Shimano Altus 8-speed gears and full suspension. The listed bike weight is 31.6 kg and the maximum load is 150 kg.

Listed range: 48 km on electric only and up to 110 km with pedal assist. Expect less in summer heat, with a heavier rider or with lots of throttle; that is an IonicRide estimate, not a manufacturer figure. The European version is limited to 25 km/h. UAE listings may differ, so check the speed setting on the product page before you order.

What stands out

  • Lightest bike in this comparison (31.6 kg listed)
  • Hydraulic disc brakes, the best brake type here
  • 150 kg listed maximum load
  • Bigger battery than the M20 and EP-2 Pro (832Wh)

Watch out for

  • No rear light listed: you will need to add one for Dubai
  • Specs above are from the European listing; confirm the UAE version
  • Still heavy to carry up stairs, even folded

Good fit for: apartment riders and heavier riders (up to 150 kg) who want hydraulic brakes and a folding frame, and who are happy to add a rear light.

ENGWE EP-2 Pro: older folding option still sold in the UAE

The EP-2 Pro is a folding e-bike with 20 × 4.0-inch fat tyres. UAE listings, including on Noon, describe a 750W motor (960W peak) and a 48V 13Ah battery. The ENGWE UAE store’s help section includes a guide to setting the speed limit to 25 km/h.

Folding helps if you live in an apartment or want to put the bike in a car boot. At around 33.5 kg (a figure from an independent review, not the UAE listing), it is still heavy to lift, so folding is about storage space more than carrying. Our guide on folding vs full-size e-bikes for Dubai apartments covers this trade-off in detail.

Important: ENGWE’s European store now lists the EP-2 Pro as no longer available and recommends the newer EP-2 Boost instead. The EP-2 Pro is still sold in the UAE, but older models can become harder to get parts for. Ask the seller how long they will stock batteries, tyres and controllers for it. If parts supply worries you, the Engine Pro 2.0 above is ENGWE’s current folding fat-tyre model.

Wind Horse V8 Pro: lowest price, but confirm the specs

The V8 Pro is the cheapest bike here at the price we saw (AED 2,785 at a UAE specialist retailer, Al Mubdie Scooters). The listing shows 20 × 4.0-inch fat tyres, a 48V 18Ah battery, front and rear suspension, disc brakes and Shimano 7-speed gears, with a weight of about 38 kg.

There is a problem with the listing. The specification list says 750W, but the description on the same page says 1000W. The listed top speed is up to 60 km/h. Before buying, ask the seller to confirm the motor rating in writing, and ask whether the speed can be limited.

A V8 Pro listing on Noon shows different figures again: 750W, a 48V 16.6Ah battery and 50 km/h. Different sellers may be selling different batches, so compare the exact listing you buy from.

Kugoo V6: most battery and load capacity, but the heaviest

According to a UAE retailer listing (Al Mubdie Scooters), the Kugoo V6 has a 1000W dual motor, a 48V 28Ah battery, dual suspension, a folding alloy frame and a 150 kg maximum load. It was priced at AED 2,955 when we checked.

The 28Ah battery (1,344Wh calculated) is more than double the M20 and EP-2 Pro, and well above the Engine Pro 2.0. The listed range is up to 60 km, which is modest for that battery size, but more realistic than many listings. The trade-offs are weight (about 48 kg) and speed (up to 50 km/h).

The listing mentions a front LED headlight but does not mention a rear light, rear reflector or bell. Dubai’s rules require all three, so check before buying or budget for adding them.

Fat tyres in UAE conditions: sand, heat and pressure

Sand. Wide tyres spread the bike’s weight over a bigger area, so they sink less in loose sand and gravel. They help on sandy edges and packed desert tracks. They will not make deep, soft dune sand easy, and riding there puts heavy load on the motor and battery.

Tyre pressure. Fat tyres run at much lower pressure than normal bike tyres. ENGWE gives a general range of about 5 to 20 PSI for fat tyres. As a rule of thumb, use the higher end on tarmac for easier rolling, and the lower end only on soft surfaces. Always stay within the range printed on the tyre sidewall. A small digital pump with a pressure readout makes this easy; the AstroAI tyre inflator on Noon or a simple tyre pressure gauge on Amazon.ae both work.

Heat. Air pressure rises as tyres get hot, and batteries age faster in high heat. Check pressure more often in summer, avoid parking the bike in direct sun, and do not charge a hot battery straight after a ride. Our guide on how Dubai heat affects e-bike batteries explains what helps.

Effort without power. Fat tyres roll with more resistance. If the battery runs out, pedalling a 32 to 48 kg fat bike home is hard work, so plan routes within your real range.

Buying checklist

  • ☐ White headlight, red tail light, red rear reflector and a bell are fitted, or you have budgeted to add them (for example a smart tail light on Noon)
  • ☐ Front and rear brakes work well (hydraulic or good mechanical discs)
  • ☐ The seller has confirmed the motor rating and top speed in writing
  • ☐ You know how to set or keep the speed limited
  • ☐ Warranty length, what it covers, and who in the UAE handles claims
  • ☐ You are buying from an authorised seller (ENGWE publishes a warning about unauthorised sellers on its website)
  • ☐ Spare 20 × 4.0 tyres, tubes and brake pads are easy to find locally
  • ☐ You can lift or store a 32 to 48 kg bike where you live
  • ☐ You have a helmet and a reflective vest: see our UAE helmet guide

FAQs

Are fat-tire e-bikes legal in Dubai?

Yes, e-bikes are a recognised type of cycle under Dubai’s Resolution No. 13 of 2022, and tyre width is not restricted. You must follow the same rules as other e-bike riders: RTA cycle lanes only, lane speed limits, required lights and bell, helmet and reflective vest, and a minimum age of 16 under the 2022 resolution.

Do I need a permit to ride an e-bike in Dubai?

The resolution requires a Rider’s Permit for e-scooters and for any other type of cycle the RTA decides. It does not list standard e-bikes. Because the RTA can extend the requirement, check the RTA’s current rules before buying a high-power model. Our guide to e-bike licences in the UAE has more detail.

Is a 1000W fat-tire e-bike allowed?

Dubai’s resolution does not set a watt limit for e-bikes. The practical limits are the lane speed limits and the ban on riding e-bikes on roads above 60 km/h. A very powerful, fast bike may also be treated differently if it looks and works like a moped, so check before buying.

Can I ride a fat-tire e-bike on the beach?

Fat tyres cope with sand better than normal tyres, but whether you are allowed to ride on a particular beach depends on local rules and signs. Check the signs at the beach before riding.

What tyre pressure should I use?

Stay within the range printed on your tyre. Fat tyres usually run between about 5 and 20 PSI. Use the higher end on tarmac and lower only on soft ground.

Bottom line

Fat tyres make an e-bike more stable and comfortable on the rough and sandy surfaces common in the UAE. But in Dubai, speed matters more than power. The bikes that are easiest to live with legally are the ones listed with a 25 km/h limit, like the ENGWE M20 and the folding ENGWE Engine Pro 2.0, which is also the lightest and the easiest to order online. The Kugoo V6 makes sense for heavier riders who need a big battery, and the Wind Horse V8 Pro is the budget option, as long as the seller confirms its specs.

Whichever you choose, confirm the lights, speed setting and warranty before you pay.
