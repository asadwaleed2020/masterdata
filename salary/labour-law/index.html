UAE labour law explained: contracts, working hours & rights

Private-sector employment law, probation, recruitment fees, breaks and worker protections.

Scope: Federal private-sector employment law, principally Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021 as amended. DIFC, ADGM, government jobs, domestic workers and pension-covered nationals can have different rules. Contractual benefits may exceed the statutory minimum. Calculators are estimates, not legal determinations.

UAE employment law: key rules

Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021 as amended is the main private-sector framework. Confirm your employment jurisdiction before using the federal rules.

Salary and minimum wage

The general federal labour law does not set a universal minimum salary for all expatriate workers. MoHRE sets a separate AED 6,000 minimum monthly wage for Emirati private-sector employees from 1 January 2026. Specific employment categories can have other requirements. Official Emirati employment guidance. Verify basic salary and cash allowances separately in your registered contract.

Contracts and probation

Probation generally cannot exceed six months. Employer termination during probation requires at least 14 days' written notice. A worker moving to another UAE employer during probation generally gives one month's notice; a worker leaving the UAE generally gives 14 days. Separate recruitment-cost rules apply between employers. Employers must not charge recruitment or employment costs to workers.

Workplace protections

The law prohibits forced labour, specified discrimination and harassment. Workers should retain a copy of their employment contract and wage records. Employers cannot retain official worker documents as a general practice. Seek advice promptly for unsafe work or disputed wage deductions.

Working hours, overtime and weekly rest

The usual private-sector ceiling is eight hours per day or 48 hours per week, with specified exceptions. Normal working hours are reduced by two hours daily during Ramadan. Employees generally must not work more than five consecutive hours without breaks totalling at least one hour; breaks ordinarily do not count as work time, subject to special arrangements.

Eligible ordinary overtime is remunerated using the basic hourly wage plus at least 25%. Eligible overtime between 10 PM and 4 AM attracts at least 50% extra; shift workers are exempt from that night premium rule. Overtime is generally limited to two hours per day, with statutory exceptions. Certain roles and work arrangements are exempt.

Working on a contractual rest day or official holiday may require a substitute day or the applicable statutory compensation. Public-holiday work under Article 28 uses normal daily wage plus at least 50% of basic daily wage if no substitute day is granted. Do not multiply every rest-day hour by 1.5 without checking the applicable rule.

Official sources: Working hours ↗ · Federal Labour Law ↗ · Leave entitlements ↗. Reviewed 27 November 2026.

Resignation, dismissal and final dues

Ordinary contractual notice must generally be 30–90 days under Article 43; the employment contract continues during notice and the employee is entitled to wage for that period. Failure to observe required notice can trigger compensation based on wage for the unserved period. Probation has separate notice rules. Summary dismissal under Article 44 requires specific grounds and procedures.

Article 53 generally requires wages and other entitlements to be settled within 14 days after the contract ends. Final dues may include outstanding wages, eligible gratuity, unused annual leave and applicable notice compensation. A valid MOHRE complaint or lawsuit can give rise to unlawful-termination protection under Article 47 where dismissal is retaliatory; compensation depends on the competent decision and may be up to three months' wage, separate from other entitlements.

Avoid signing an acknowledgement that you received amounts you have not actually received. Request an itemised settlement and keep copies of the contract, wage records and correspondence.
