Long-term illness in UAE: salary, job & insurance

What happens to pay, employment and health coverage if you cannot work for months due to illness.

Scope: Federal private-sector employment law, principally Federal Decree-Law 33 of 2021 as amended. DIFC, ADGM, government jobs, domestic workers and pension-covered nationals can have different rules. Contractual benefits may exceed the statutory minimum. Calculators are estimates, not legal determinations.

If you cannot work for 1, 3, 6 or 12 months

ScenarioOrdinary illnessQualifying work injury
First 30 days15 full + 15 half (if unused)Full wage during treatment
Days 31–45Half wageFull wage during treatment
Days 46–90Unpaid statutory sick leaveFull wage during treatment
Months 4–6No automatic further sick payFull wage, up to six months
Months 7–12No automatic further sick payHalf wage, subject to earlier end

Illustration assumes no ordinary sick leave already used and a continuous incapacity. For cancer, major surgery, cardiac illness or another serious condition unrelated to work, there is no automatic separate six-month or one-year paid leave under Article 31. A diagnosis alone does not establish occupational disease.

Practical plan

  • Obtain a medical report and notify HR promptly. Ask for written confirmation of the sick days used, remaining entitlement and expected payroll.
  • Check your health insurer's network, approvals, annual limits and whether any separate group disability or critical-illness cover exists.
  • Discuss possible return-to-work arrangements, annual leave, approved unpaid leave or enhanced company benefits. Do not presume these are granted.
  • If employment may end, request a written decision, itemised final settlement, insurance cancellation date and applicable visa information. Contact the relevant labour authority before signing disputed documents.

How employee medical insurance works

Employer-provided medical insurance pays for eligible treatment under the policy, not automatically for lost wages. From January 2025 the UAE expanded mandatory coverage to the remaining emirates for private-sector employees and domestic workers through residency issuance/renewal requirements, alongside existing Dubai and Abu Dhabi rules. Check the policy and relevant emirate regulations; dependent coverage is not identical everywhere.

Before visiting a hospital or clinic

  • Check your insurer, policy number, expiry date, approved network and whether your hospital accepts direct billing.
  • For non-emergency planned care, ask about referrals, prior authorisation, deductible, co-payment, annual benefit limit, pharmacy benefits and exclusions.
  • For urgent or emergency symptoms seek medical assistance promptly; do not delay necessary emergency care for an insurance approval. Confirm claim procedures with your insurer afterward.
  • Keep medical reports, invoices, prescriptions and written approval or denial. Ask the insurer for the specific policy clause if a claim is rejected.

Serious illness and chronic conditions

The federal basic package introduced for the remaining emirates is advertised at AED 320 per year with no waiting period for chronic illnesses; its benefits and cost-sharing rules are package-specific. It is not a universal description of every Dubai or Abu Dhabi plan. The government describes a 20% inpatient co-payment and medication cost-sharing capped at 30% with an annual medication cap of AED 1,500 for that package. Check the full benefit limits and current policy schedule before planned treatment.

What if your employment or residency ends?

Do not assume coverage continues for the entire treatment or visa grace period. Request the exact cancellation/expiry date, continuity options, network and authorisation status in writing from HR and the insurer. Arrange replacement coverage where required; rules and grace periods differ. Health insurance, employer sick pay, occupational injury compensation and unemployment insurance are distinct.
