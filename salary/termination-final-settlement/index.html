Resignation, dismissal and final dues

Ordinary contractual notice must generally be 30–90 days under Article 43; the employment contract continues during notice and the employee is entitled to wage for that period. Failure to observe required notice can trigger compensation based on wage for the unserved period. Probation has separate notice rules. Summary dismissal under Article 44 requires specific grounds and procedures.

Article 53 generally requires wages and other entitlements to be settled within 14 days after the contract ends. Final dues may include outstanding wages, eligible gratuity, unused annual leave and applicable notice compensation. A valid MOHRE complaint or lawsuit can give rise to unlawful-termination protection under Article 47 where dismissal is retaliatory; compensation depends on the competent decision and may be up to three months' wage, separate from other entitlements.

Avoid signing an acknowledgement that you received amounts you have not actually received. Request an itemised settlement and keep copies of the contract, wage records and correspondence.
