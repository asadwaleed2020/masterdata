Recognised workplace injury and occupational disease — Article 37

For a qualifying injury or occupational disease, the employer is responsible for treatment costs until recovery or disability is established, under the applicable rules. If the condition prevents work, full wage is payable during treatment for up to six months. If treatment continues, half wage may be payable for a further six months or until recovery, established disability or death, whichever occurs first.

Disability and death compensation

Separate statutory compensation may apply. In a qualifying death case, the formula is 24 months of basic wage, subject to AED 18,000 minimum and AED 200,000 maximum. Disability compensation depends on the medical assessment and statutory schedules; the treatment-pay calculator does not estimate permanent disability.

What to do after a workplace injury

  • Seek appropriate medical treatment and tell your supervisor immediately.
  • Request the incident report and keep treatment reports, witness details and any official reports.
  • Ask the employer to follow required reporting procedures with the competent authorities. Do not assume an ordinary medical certificate alone establishes occupational causation.
  • If there is a dispute about classification, treatment bills or pay, contact MOHRE or the applicable labour authority.

Frequently asked questions

Does a normal illness qualify?

Not automatically. The condition must qualify as work injury or occupational disease.

Does the employer pay treatment costs?

For qualifying work injuries, employer obligations include treatment costs under the applicable law.

Is six months of full wage guaranteed regardless of recovery?

No. The period ends earlier when statutory conditions for ending treatment pay are met.
