Electric Scooter Age Limit in the UAE: Can Kids Ride E-Scooters in Dubai? (2026)

⏱ 10 min read 📅 Updated October 2026 By Alex at IonicRide

In Dubai, riders must be at least 17 to get the RTA e-scooter permit — and adult supervision does not create an exception. Not “just inside the community,” not for slow riding. Under-17s can’t get a permit, so they can’t legally ride e-scooters in Dubai’s public spaces. (Older guides say 16 — that was the original 2022 rule.)

This matters more in 2026 than ever: enforcement patrols now actively stop underage riders, scooters are confiscated, and — the part many parents don’t realise — UAE courts have pursued negligence cases against parents after accidents involving minors on e-scooters.

This guide covers the exact age rules, what genuinely happens when a child is caught, where younger kids can legally ride, and the safe, legal path for teenagers who turn 17.

17 — for riding e-scooters in any public space in the UAE.

Teens can get the RTA permit online and ride legally in designated zones — helmet on, 20 km/h max.

What the Law Actually Says

Dubai’s personal-mobility rules started with Executive Council Resolution No. 13 of 2022, which regulates bicycles, e-bikes and e-scooters across the emirate and originally set the minimum riding age at 16. When RTA moved the e-scooter permit fully online (announced by Dubai Media Office in December 2025), the published requirement became 17 years and above. Riders who hold a valid UAE or international driving licence don’t need the separate permit.

ℹ️ Age rule update — January 2026

Many websites (and earlier versions of this guide) still say 16. Current RTA permit guidance, as reported by Gulf News and Gulf Business in January 2026, says applicants must be 17 or older. Confirm on the official RTA website or app before your teenager rides.

Outside Dubai, emirates publish much less detail about age. Don’t assume Dubai’s rule applies everywhere — each emirate runs its own framework, and some restrict where scooters can be ridden at all.

Three details parents consistently miss:

  • “Public space” is broader than you think. Streets, cycle tracks, beach promenades, parks, and — crucially — the internal roads of residential communities all count. The quiet street outside your villa is not private property.
  • There is no junior permit. The RTA permit starts at 17. Nothing exists below that age, so there’s no paperwork route to make a 13-year-old’s riding legal.
  • The age rule sits on top of everything else. Even at 17+, riders need the permit (or a valid driving licence), a helmet, and to stay in designated zones — the full picture is in our Dubai e-scooter laws guide.

⚠️ The Community-Street Myth

A common assumption among parents: “it’s fine, they only ride inside our community.” Community streets function as public roads — security teams stop underage riders at gates, hold scooters, and escalate repeat cases to police. If your community’s own rules also ban it (many now do), you’re breaking two sets of rules at once.

Why the UAE Enforces This So Hard in 2026

This isn’t bureaucratic box-ticking — it’s a response to real numbers. Official figures reported for 2024 put Dubai at 254 accidents involving e-scooters and bicycles, with 10 deaths and 259 injuries. Worse, recent e-scooter fatalities have disproportionately involved minors — deaths reported in 2025 included children as young as 9 and 15. And in one widely reported case, the parents of an injured 13-year-old rider were taken to court for negligence after she was hurt crossing a road on her e-scooter.

That’s the backdrop for the 2026 escalation: since May 1, 2026, the joint RTA–Dubai Police Personal Mobility Monitoring Unit patrols riding areas across the city, and underage riding is one of the behaviours it specifically targets. Residents also report unsafe and underage riding directly through Dubai Police channels — so enforcement doesn’t depend on a patrol happening to pass by. Our coverage of Dubai’s 2026 e-scooter crackdown has the full picture.

Parent Liability: What You’re Actually Exposed To

Here’s the section that changes minds. When a minor rides an e-scooter illegally in the UAE, the legal and financial exposure lands on the parents:

  • Responsibility for the violation. Authorities have made clear that parents are held responsible when minors ride illegally — the fine and the consequences are yours, not the child’s.
  • Negligence proceedings. Courts have pursued negligence cases against parents after accidents involving children on e-scooters — including when the child was the one injured. Knowingly allowing the riding strengthens that case.
  • Full financial exposure in an accident. Illegal riding is generally outside any insurance protection. If your child injures a pedestrian, damages a vehicle, or is injured themselves, the costs sit with the family.
  • Confiscation. The scooter itself can be impounded — and recovering an impounded ride involves documents, fees and time, as our guide to e-scooter confiscation in Dubai explains.
🚫 The Question That Settles It

Would you let your 13-year-old drive the family car around the community “because it’s quiet”? An e-scooter at 25 km/h next to moving SUVs is closer to that than to a toy. The law treats it accordingly — and after an accident, so will a court.

Where Kids Under 17 CAN Legally Ride

None of this means childhood is cancelled. The legal space for younger kids is real — it’s just genuinely private:

  • Private property with permission — a villa garden, a private farm or compound. This is the clean legal answer.
  • Low-speed kids’ toy scooters, not adult e-scooters. Small, speed-limited children’s models used on private ground are a different world from a full-size e-scooter on a street. Our roundup of the best electric scooters for kids in Dubai covers which models are genuinely age-appropriate — and where they should and shouldn’t be used.
  • Supervised, always — and helmet on, always.
  • Regular bicycles remain a great option. Kids on pedal bikes on community cycle paths, with helmets, are in a far friendlier legal and safety position — and it builds the skills that make them better e-scooter riders at 17.
💡 The Bridge Strategy That Works

The families who handle this well treat 17 as a milestone, not an obstacle: pedal bike now, riding skills and road sense built early, then the RTA permit on the 17th birthday and a sensible first e-scooter. The teen gets independence with a licence-style moment attached to it — which can also make them more rule-conscious riders.

Turning 17: The Legal Path for Teen Riders

At 17, everything opens up — legally and simply:

  • Get the RTA permit. Online application, short training, simple test, digital permit. Our step-by-step permit guide with screenshots takes about 20 minutes.
  • Choose a sensible first scooter. Dual brakes, controlled speed, good lights, manageable weight — not the highest top speed the budget allows. Our guide to the best student scooters under AED 2,000 is built for exactly this buyer.
  • Fit a real helmet. It’s mandatory, it’s the most common fine, and it’s the difference-maker in the accident statistics. See our Dubai helmet guide.
  • Teach the zone rules before the first ride. Designated areas, 20 km/h, no passengers, dismount at crossings — the fines are in our complete fines breakdown, and they apply to 17-year-olds in full.

Parent Safety Checklist

Whether your child is 8 on a toy scooter in the garden or 17 with a fresh permit, here is a practical checklist for parents:

  • ✓ Right machine for the age — toy-class, speed-limited models for under-17s on private ground; never an adult e-scooter “set to slow mode.”
  • ✓ Helmet every single ride — non-negotiable at every age, on every surface.
  • ✓ Private property only under 17 — and confirm your community’s own rules in writing if riding anywhere near shared space.
  • ✓ Supervision for younger kids — unsupervised riding is where small mistakes turn into injuries.
  • ✓ Brakes checked monthly — kids’ scooters get thrown around; brake cables and pads suffer first.
  • ✓ Charge safely — certified charger, never overnight unattended, never blocking an exit. Our apartment charging safety guide applies to kids’ models too.
  • ✓ At 17: permit first, scooter second — make the RTA permit the condition for the purchase.
  • ✓ Model the behaviour — kids ride the way their parents ride. Helmet on, zones respected, phone away.

The Bottom Line for Parents

📋 The Bottom Line

17 is the line, and it’s enforced. Under-17s cannot ride e-scooters in any UAE public space — community streets included — and supervision changes nothing.

The consequences land on you, not the child. Confiscation, fines, and in accident cases, negligence proceedings against parents are all on the table in 2026.

There’s a good legal path at every age. Toy scooters on private ground and pedal bikes for the younger years; the RTA permit and a sensible first scooter at 17. Treat the 17th birthday as the licence moment and everyone wins.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What is the minimum age to ride an electric scooter in the UAE?

In Dubai, 17. Current RTA permit rules (online service announced December 2025) require applicants to be 17 or older, and holders of a valid UAE or international driving licence don’t need the separate permit. The original 2022 rules set 16, so older guides still quote that. Other emirates publish less detail — check locally.

❓ Can a 13-year-old ride an electric scooter in Dubai?

No. Under-17s cannot legally ride e-scooters in public spaces — streets, tracks, parks, promenades and community roads all count. Supervision doesn’t create an exception, and no permit exists under 17. The legal option is a low-speed kids’ toy scooter on private property, supervised.

❓ Can my child ride if I’m supervising them?

No — the 17+ rule applies regardless of adult presence. A parent knowingly allowing underage riding can actually strengthen a negligence case if something goes wrong.

❓ Can parents be punished if their child rides an e-scooter?

Yes. Parents are held responsible when minors ride illegally, and UAE courts have pursued negligence cases against parents after accidents involving children on e-scooters. Add confiscation, fines, and uninsured financial exposure if the child injures someone, and the risk sits squarely with the family.

❓ What happens if a child is caught riding in Dubai?

The rider is stopped, the scooter can be confiscated, and parents are contacted and held responsible. Since May 2026, dedicated RTA–Police patrols target underage riding specifically, and residents report cases through police channels. Repeat or accident cases can escalate to court.

❓ Are kids’ electric scooters legal in the UAE?

Owning and selling low-speed children’s toy scooters is legal — the restriction is where they’re used. Public riding requires the rider to be 17+; kids’ models belong on genuinely private property, supervised, with a helmet.

❓ Where can kids under 17 legally ride?

On private property with the owner’s permission and adult supervision — villa gardens, private compounds, dedicated kids’ zones where operators allow it. Community streets don’t count as private. For public riding, the path is: turn 17, get the RTA permit.

❓ Is the age limit the same in Abu Dhabi and Sharjah?

Not necessarily. 17+ is Dubai’s RTA permit requirement. Other emirates run their own rules and publish less detail on age, and some restrict where scooters can be used at all. Check your emirate’s current rules if you’re outside Dubai.

❓ Can a 17-year-old get the RTA permit?

Yes — 17 is exactly when the permit becomes available. Online application with Emirates ID details, short training, simple test, digital permit. A 17-year-old with the permit rides legally in designated zones with a helmet at up to 20 km/h.

❓ What’s a safe first e-scooter for a 17-year-old?

Prioritise dual brakes, controlled top speed, good lights and manageable weight over performance claims. Lightweight budget-to-mid commuter models are the sensible start, paired with a proper helmet — avoid anything marketed on speed.
