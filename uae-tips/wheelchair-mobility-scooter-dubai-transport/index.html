Dubai Public Transport with a Wheelchair or Mobility Scooter: Metro, Buses and Taxis

Byionicwaleed November 3, 2026November 3, 2026

Quick answer

RTA says the Metro, Tram and buses have dedicated wheelchair spaces, and Dubai Taxi offers People of Determination vehicles, with or without a lift, on request. We found no published size limit for wheelchairs, and no statement that mobility scooters are accepted. So ask RTA or Dubai Taxi about your exact device before you travel. The 20 kg and 120 x 70 x 40 cm rule for e-scooters is not a wheelchair rule.

How this guide was made: We read RTA and Dubai Taxi pages, and dated reports, on 3 November 2026. IonicRide has not tested these journeys. Services can change, and some older information may be out of date, so we show dates. “Not confirmed” means we did not find a clear published rule. Call RTA on 800 9090 if you are unsure.

A wheelchair is not an e-scooter

RTA’s rules for carrying e-scooters are for recreational scooters. Do not use their folded-size, weight, seat or folding rules to decide about a powered wheelchair. In the other direction, do not assume wheelchair spaces cover a mobility scooter. For recreational scooters, see our Metro guide.

Metro

RTA’s People of Determination page lists:

  • A designated wheelchair space in each carriage, near the door
  • Lifts with ample space, and spacious doors
  • Ticket booths designed for wheelchair users
  • Accessible toilets and parking near main entrances

A wheelchair traveller’s guide, by a power wheelchair user, describes riding in Silver Class, and says to lock your wheels. It was last updated in February 2025. Ask RTA about any gap between train and platform for your wheel size, and about securement.

Tram

RTA’s tram page says each train has dedicated spaces for wheelchair users, and that each train and station has an emergency call box. It gives no device sizes. Check whether the station’s entry and your route suit your device.

Buses

  • RTA’s bus page says all buses have dedicated spaces for wheelchair users.
  • RTA’s People of Determination page refers to new buses with lowered floors. A 2020 report described 1,500 buses that lower towards the kerb.
  • The wheelchair traveller’s guide describes ramps and warns about thin kerbs at some stops. We did not find RTA’s own wheelchair ramp details.
  • Gilani Mobility, a Dubai supplier, says most buses let manual and electric wheelchairs board, but it does not give sources or mention scooters.

Ask about your route, as the stop matters as much as the bus. Check the kerb and shelter at both ends.

Taxis

Dubai Taxi’s page says:

  • Booking: call centre on +971 4 208 0190 or 800 88088, at any time. Also the DTC app and Bolt, and the street or airport stands.
  • Vehicles: larger vehicles with a red roof and a blue icon, with or without a lift on request.
  • Fares: AED 8 by day, AED 9 at night, and AED 2.19 per km. Airport and port trips have a AED 25 base fare and AED 2.36 per km. Salik applies.
  • Discount: a Sanad card gives 50% off.

The page does not mention electric wheelchairs or scooters, so say what you have when you book. RTA’s page mentions 5 taxis, a figure that may be out of date. Do not assume availability at short notice, and book ahead if you can. For passengers with non-motor disabilities, Dubai Taxi announced in January 2024 a booking option for regular taxis.

What to ask when booking

  • ☐ Do you have a vehicle with a lift or ramp? What are its width, height and weight limits?
  • ☐ Can my device be fixed safely, and how?
  • ☐ Do I stay in my device, or transfer to a seat?
  • ☐ Can it carry my device’s battery type?
  • ☐ Can a companion ride, and is there a return booking?

Water transport

RTA says the water bus can take up to 3 wheelchairs, and Dubai Ferry 2. Ask about boarding and the ramp at your station.

Fares and help

  • Nol card: RTA lists a personalised nol card for People of Determination at AED 70, giving free travel on the Metro and public buses for card holders, valid for 5 years. A 2020 report also mentioned fare exemptions on the tram and marine transport, so check.
  • Journey planning: Khaleej Times reported in October 2026 that RTA’s S’hail app has voice-based help to plan and book trips.
  • Emergency wheelchairs: the same report says these are available across RTA facilities, if your own chair is damaged or you become tired.
  • Contacts: RTA 800 9090, ask@rta.ae, or its Mahboub chat. Dubai Taxi 800 88088.

Measure your device

Have these ready before you call, with the rider seated, if that changes the size.

  • ☐ Overall length, width and height
  • ☐ Weight, with and without the rider
  • ☐ Turning radius, from the manual
  • ☐ Ground clearance and wheel size, for gaps and ramps
  • ☐ Battery type and rating, from the label
  • ☐ Whether the device has a transit-approved tie-down point, from the manual
  • ☐ Whether you can transfer, if needed

RTA did not publish wheelchair limits on the pages we read, so your numbers help them give you a real answer. If you are still choosing, see our guides to electric wheelchairs and mobility scooters.

Mobility scooters: what we can say

RTA pages we read talk about wheelchairs, not mobility scooters. They do not say scooters are refused or accepted. A scooter’s length, turning space and tiller could make boarding harder than a wheelchair of the same width. That is our reading, not an RTA rule. Do not set out on a mobility scooter without an answer for your exact model and route, and have a taxi or other backup ready.

If something goes wrong

We did not find a public page showing lift outages in real time. So:

  • ☐ Check the route and station with RTA before you travel.
  • ☐ Ask about lift faults at both stations, and the next accessible station.
  • ☐ Save RTA, Dubai Taxi and a companion’s numbers.
  • ☐ Keep your device charged, with extra range for detours.
  • ☐ Allow extra time, and avoid peak hours if you can.
  • ☐ If you are stuck, tell station staff, or use an emergency call point on the tram.
  • ☐ Carry your medical and device details, in case someone needs to help.

Heat matters too. Plan shaded waits and extra water, especially in summer.

FAQs

Can I take my electric wheelchair on the Dubai Metro?

RTA describes wheelchair spaces, lifts and wide doors, and doesn’t exclude electric wheelchairs on the pages we read. We found no size limit either, so check your device with RTA.

Does the 20 kg limit apply to my wheelchair?

That limit is in RTA’s rules for e-scooters. We found nothing that applies it to wheelchairs. Ask RTA if a station tells you otherwise.

Can I charge my device at a station?

A 2020 report mentioned electric wheelchair charging at Jebel Ali, Deira, Hatta and Al Qusais bus stations. Confirm these first, and do not rely on charging at a station.

Can tourists use these services?

Dubai Taxi’s 2024 announcement mentions residents, visitors and tourists. Ask about discounts, as a Sanad or nol card may have its own rules.

Bottom line

Dubai’s main transport lines describe wheelchair access, but the published details are general. Measure your device, call RTA or Dubai Taxi, and plan a backup. For mobility scooters, get an answer for your exact model before you go.
