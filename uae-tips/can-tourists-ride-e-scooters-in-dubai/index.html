Can Tourists Ride E-Scooters in Dubai? Rentals, Permit & Rules (2026)

⏱ 11 min read 📅 Updated July 2026 By Alex at IonicRide

Yes — tourists can ride e-scooters in Dubai in 2026. You need to meet the UAE e-scooter age limit of 17 or older, wear a helmet, stay inside the designated zones, keep to 20 km/h, and hold either a free RTA e-scooter permit or a valid driving licence the RTA recognises (a UAE licence or an international driving licence works instead of the permit).

Rentals are everywhere in visitor areas like Dubai Marina, JBR and Downtown — you unlock a shared scooter through an app for roughly AED 3 plus around AED 0.5–1 per minute, so a short beachfront ride usually costs AED 8–18.

The one thing that changed for 2026: since May 1, joint RTA–Dubai Police patrols actively check riders in exactly the areas tourists ride. The rules were always there — now they’re enforced. This guide covers everything: the permit, the apps, the zones, the fines, and the mistakes visitors actually make.

Is It Legal for Tourists to Ride E-Scooters in Dubai?

Completely legal — Dubai actively wants visitors on e-scooters. The RTA has approved shared rental operators, built hundreds of kilometres of tracks, and placed most of the rental fleet exactly where tourists go: the Marina, JBR, Downtown, and along the beach tracks. The rules come from Dubai’s personal-mobility framework (Executive Council Resolution No. 13 of 2022), and they apply to everyone on a scooter — visitor or resident, rental or owned.

The core requirements are simple:

  • Age 17+ — no exceptions, and parents can be held legally responsible if minors ride.
  • Helmet mandatory — this is the fine tourists get most often, because rental scooters don’t come with one.
  • 20 km/h speed limit in designated areas (rental scooters are electronically capped anyway).
  • Designated zones only — cycling tracks, e-scooter tracks and approved districts. No highways, no main roads, no crowded pedestrian-only pavements.
  • One rider per scooter — no passengers, no children standing in front of you. Tandem riding is specifically targeted by the 2026 patrols.
  • Dismount at pedestrian crossings — walk the scooter across.
  • Permit or recognised licence — covered in the next section.
⚠️ What Changed in 2026

From May 1, 2026, a joint RTA–Dubai Police Personal Mobility Monitoring Unit patrols cycling tracks, soft-mobility lanes and key districts — checking helmets, speed, tandem riding and where you’re riding. Non-compliant scooters can be impounded on the spot. The patrols concentrate on high-traffic areas like the Marina, JBR and Downtown — in other words, exactly where tourists ride. How Tourists Get the Permit (Passport, Not Emirates ID)

The RTA’s online application accepts visitors — you don’t need an Emirates ID. Here’s the tourist version of the process:

  1. Go to the RTA’s official e-scooter permit page (rta.ae). Choose the option for non-residents.
  2. Register with your passport number or UAE visit-visa details, plus a working mobile number and email address. A local tourist SIM makes this smoother.
  3. Complete the short online training — safety videos and traffic-sign basics for e-scooter riders.
  4. Pass the short test — it’s simple if you watched the material.
  5. Receive your digital permit by email. Save it on your phone and keep it accessible whenever you ride.

💡 Do It Before You Fly

The permit application is fully online, so you can complete it from your hotel — or from home before your trip. Ten minutes on the plane’s Wi-Fi beats arguing with a patrol officer at JBR. Keep both the permit and your passport photo page saved offline on your phone.

How to Rent an E-Scooter in Dubai (Apps, Prices, How It Works)

Shared e-scooters in Dubai are run by RTA-approved operators — the lineup has shifted over the years (Tier became Dott internationally, and local operators like Arnab and Skurtt operate alongside Lime), so check which apps show scooters in your area rather than downloading all of them. The mechanics are identical across operators:

  1. Download the operator’s app and register with your phone number and a card. No cash payments — Apple Pay and Google Pay work on the bigger apps.
  2. Open the map — it shows available scooters, battery levels, and crucially the green riding zones. If the map looks empty, zoom out.
  3. Scan the QR code on the handlebar to unlock.
  4. Do a 10-second check — squeeze both brakes, glance at the tyres, check the battery percentage (skip anything under ~30%).
  5. End the ride in a designated parking zone, take the photo the app asks for, and confirm the ride has ended before walking away.

Staying More Than a Few Days? Do the Maths

App rentals are perfect for occasional rides, but the per-minute model punishes heavy use. If you’ll ride daily for a week or more, a private weekly rental from a scooter shop (flat rate, no zone-availability lottery, often includes a helmet) usually beats app pricing. For stays of a month-plus, even buying a budget scooter can win — a usable commuter starts around AED 600–1,500, and our true cost of ownership breakdown shows where the crossover sits. Just remember: a rental from a shop or a scooter you own still needs the permit and still follows zone rules.

Where Tourists Can (and Can’t) Ride

Dubai allows e-scooters in designated districts and on dedicated cycling and e-scooter tracks — and conveniently, the approved list includes almost every area tourists actually visit. The rental app maps are the easiest compass: if the app lets you ride there, it’s a legal zone; if the scooter slows to a crawl or the app warns you, turn back.

The Best Legal Rides for Visitors

  • Dubai Marina & JBR — the classic. Flat, scenic, dense with rental scooters, and busy with patrols, so follow the rules here especially.
  • Kite Beach & the Jumeirah beach tracks — dedicated tracks with sea views; best at sunrise and sunset.
  • Downtown & Boulevard area — great for short hops between Dubai Mall, the fountain area and cafés.
  • Dubai Canal — a quieter waterfront ride connecting Business Bay toward Jumeirah.
  • Purpose-built tracks like Al Qudra (cycling) if you’re feeling ambitious — though that’s more a bike outing than a scooter hop.

Where You’ll Get Fined

  • Highways and main roads — never, under any circumstances.
  • Crowded pedestrian-only pavements — including the busiest stretches of The Walk at JBR at peak evening times; watch for signage.
  • Outside the app’s green zone — rental scooters often auto-limit or stop, and you can still be fined.
  • Malls, souks and metro station interiors — walk it or park it.

Fines apply to visitors exactly as they do to residents, and they’re issued on the spot. Reported penalties generally fall in the AED 100–500 range depending on the violation. These are the ones we hear about most from tourists:

🚫 Don’t Ignore a Fine and Fly Home

Fines in the UAE are linked to your identity documents, and unpaid penalties can surface at awkward moments — like your next visa application or entry. If you’re fined, pay it or contest it properly before you leave. It’s a 10-minute job online.

Can You Take an E-Scooter on the Dubai Metro?

Since late 2024, foldable standing e-scooters are allowed on the Dubai Metro and Tram — useful if you’ve rented privately or bought one. The limits are strict: the scooter must fold, must have no seat, must fit within roughly 120cm × 70cm × 40cm folded, and must weigh 20kg or less. It has to stay folded the entire time inside stations and trains.

Shared app rentals are a different story — you can’t carry a Lime or Arnab scooter onto the Metro, and ending your ride requires a designated parking zone anyway. Full details in our guide to taking e-scooters on the Dubai Metro.

The Summer Heat Reality (June–September)

If you’re visiting between June and September, plan rides around the heat. Afternoon temperatures regularly pass 40°C, the handlebars of a scooter parked in the sun can be genuinely painful to hold, and battery performance drops in extreme heat. The pattern that works:

  • Ride 7–10am or after 6pm — this is also when scooter availability is best and the beachfront is at its nicest.
  • Carry water even for a 20-minute ride.
  • Check battery percentage before unlocking — heat-stressed batteries drain faster than the app estimates.
  • Winter visitors (Nov–Mar) can ignore all of this — daytime riding is perfect.
📌 Local Knowledge

Dubai’s riding culture is nocturnal in summer. The beach tracks at 8pm are busier than at 2pm — join the locals. Evening rides also mean more patrols, so helmet on.

7 Mistakes Tourists Make on Dubai E-Scooters

🚫 Seen Every Week — Workshop & Marina Experience

Riding two-up for the photo — tandem riding is a targeted violation in 2026, and it’s the easiest fine to collect at JBR.

Skipping the helmet — “the rental didn’t come with one” is not a defence. Pack one or buy one.

Assuming anywhere walkable is rideable — crowded pedestrian pavements are off-limits even inside legal districts.

Not ending the ride properly — walk away without confirming and the meter keeps running. Screenshot the ride-end screen.

Parking badly — outside the app’s parking zone or blocking a path means an automatic app penalty.

Riding at 2pm in July — heat exhaustion is a real risk. Mornings and evenings only in summer.

Ignoring a fine before flying out — settle it online before departure; it takes minutes.

Final Verdict: Should Tourists Ride E-Scooters in Dubai?

Absolutely — for short hops inside the Marina, JBR, Downtown and along the beach tracks, an e-scooter is the cheapest, fastest and most enjoyable way for a visitor to move around. The rules are genuinely easy to follow once you know them, and everything you need (the permit, the app, the map of legal zones) is on your phone in under half an hour.

📋 The Bottom Line

Yes, tourists can ride — legally and easily. Get the free RTA permit online with your passport (or carry your international driving licence), wear a helmet, and stay inside the app’s green zones.

Budget AED 3 + ~0.5–1/min for app rentals, switch to a weekly private rental if you’ll ride daily, and always end your ride properly in a parking zone.

Respect the 2026 enforcement. Patrols now actively cover exactly the areas tourists ride. One rider per scooter, helmet on, 20 km/h — follow those three and you’ll never meet them.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Can tourists ride e-scooters in Dubai?

Yes. Tourists can rent and ride e-scooters in Dubai’s approved zones. You must be 17 or older, wear a helmet, keep to the 20 km/h limit, and hold either a free RTA e-scooter permit or a recognised driving licence (UAE or international). Rules are actively enforced, so stick to designated areas.

❓ Do tourists need a permit to ride an e-scooter in Dubai?

Yes, unless you hold a valid UAE driving licence or an international driving licence recognised by Dubai authorities. Otherwise, apply for the free RTA permit online using your passport or visit-visa details, a mobile number and an email. Short online lessons and a simple test — around 20 minutes total.

❓ How much does it cost to rent an e-scooter in Dubai?

Roughly AED 3 to unlock plus around AED 0.5–1 per minute, depending on operator and pricing at the time. A short 10–15 minute ride usually lands around AED 8–18. Heavy daily riders should compare weekly private rentals, which skip per-minute fees.

❓ Where can tourists ride e-scooters in Dubai?

Only in designated areas — dedicated tracks and approved districts, which include Dubai Marina, JBR, Downtown, Kite Beach and canal-side routes. Highways, main roads and crowded pedestrian-only pavements are off-limits and fined. The rental app’s map shows the legal green zones.

❓ What are the e-scooter fines in Dubai for tourists?

The same as for residents — commonly AED 100–500 depending on the violation: no helmet, no permit, wrong zone, speeding, or carrying a passenger. Since May 2026, dedicated RTA–Police patrols enforce these in person and can impound non-compliant scooters.

❓ Can I take an e-scooter on the Dubai Metro?

Foldable standing e-scooters (no seat) have been allowed on the Metro and Tram since late 2024, within limits of roughly 120×70×40cm folded and 20kg maximum, and they must stay folded inside stations and trains. Shared app rentals can’t be taken aboard.

❓ Is Uber or an e-scooter cheaper for tourists?

For 1–3 km hops inside a zone like Marina or Downtown, the e-scooter usually wins on price and often on time. For longer cross-city trips, taxis, ride-hailing or the Metro make more sense, since scooters are zone-limited and capped at 20 km/h.

❓ What is the minimum age to ride an e-scooter in Dubai?

17 — with no exceptions for tourists, and parents can be held legally responsible if minors ride. Under-17s cannot legally ride e-scooters in public areas even with adult supervision.

❓ Is it safe to ride an e-scooter in Dubai summer?

Midday summer rides are genuinely unpleasant and can be unsafe above 40°C. Ride early morning or after sunset from June to September, carry water, and check scooter battery levels before unlocking. Winter visitors can ride comfortably all day.

❓ Should a tourist rent or buy an e-scooter in Dubai?

Under a week with occasional rides: app rentals. A week or more of daily riding: a flat-rate weekly rental from a shop. A month or longer: buying a budget scooter (from around AED 600–1,500) can win — just remember the permit and zone rules apply either way.

Tourist Pre-Ride Checklist — 60 Seconds

  • ✓ Permit saved on your phone (or international driving licence in your pocket)
  • ✓ Helmet on — the most common tourist fine in Dubai
  • ✓ You’re inside the app’s green zone and staying there
  • ✓ One rider only — no sharing, no kids on the deck
  • ✓ Brakes squeezed, tyres glanced at, battery above ~30%
  • ✓ Summer? It’s before 10am or after 6pm, and you have water
  • ✓ Ride ended properly in a parking zone, with the confirmation screen
  • ✓ Any fine paid or contested online before your flight home
